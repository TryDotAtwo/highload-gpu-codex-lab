# Beautiful Science: choose a research path

Beauty can suggest where to look. A research decision rests on what the path can explain, establish, distinguish, or make possible under the actual constraints. This is a decision aid, not a truth detector or an empirically validated discovery algorithm.

## Start from the decision

Identify the object being judged, the user's goal, the exact claim and domain, the available evidence, and the decision that is needed now. Reuse facts already available. Ask only for missing information that changes that decision. Routine execution of an already justified step does not require a full review.

Choose the relevant role, without spawning another agent automatically:
- **Investigator:** turn an attractive idea into a claim, expose its weakest bridge, and propose the next informative result
- **Supervisor:** audit the claim against raw evidence, compare a credible alternative, and decide where the next work is justified

Use [roles and stopping](references/roles-and-stopping.md) for delegation, stalled work, disagreement, and resource decisions.

## Make the scientific promise explicit

Keep four things distinguishable: aesthetic preference, established properties, empirical support, and expected research payoff. A beautiful explanation generated after seeing the answer is another claim to check.

Inspect only the axes that can change this decision, using [checks](references/checks.md):
- explanatory force, genuine unification, conditional necessity, and generativity
- testability and predictions not already used to select the candidate
- assumptions, quantifiers, regimes, boundary cases, and transfer limits
- what survives an equivalent representation and what was only hidden in notation
- full cost of obtaining, using, checking, and interpreting the result

These are independent considerations, not numbers to average. Preserve incomparability when the goal does not settle the trade-off. Complexity, ugliness, elapsed time, or a failed variant alone does not justify abandoning a productive program.

## Give a check the power to change the decision

Name a strong alternative, a minimal counterexample or invariant where appropriate, and the smallest reliable observation, proof obligation, or experiment that distinguishes the live possibilities. Specify possible outcomes before running it. Check whether the proposed test can actually discriminate at available precision; exact observational equivalence cannot be cured by repetition.

For proofs, finite searches, empirical falsification, and verifier independence, read [evidence and tests](references/evidence-and-tests.md). Keep a broken proof separate from a false theorem, and a rejected prediction separate from a uniquely established replacement.

Derive effort from the needed evidence: the unresolved lemma, resource bound, detectable effect, or validated precision requirement. If these are unknown, make estimation or formalization the next task. Repeating an unchanged benchmark is justified only by a named uncertainty the repetitions can resolve. A deadline limits what can be done; it does not strengthen the conclusion.

## Return a decision, not a ritual

Recommend one of: **continue**, **bounded test**, **change method**, or **park this claim/path**. Name exactly what changes and what survives. A recommendation to stop a scientific line does not itself authorize cancelling jobs, deleting artifacts, or changing other work.

Give a short [decision memo](references/decision-memo.md), in the user's language. Always include **evidence_status** for the decisive claims, their scope, the next discriminating result, and the condition that would change your recommendation. Use ordinary prose when the decision is small; expand only for material uncertainty or consequences.

When useful, consult [examples](references/examples.md), [historical counterweights](references/history-and-sources.md), or [optional integrations](references/integrations.md). No other plugin is required.

## Common traps

- “Elegant, therefore true” → identify the claim and its independent support
- “Long, therefore bad” → inspect obligations, progress, and full cost
- “One failed model kills the idea” → preserve the exact object of refutation and useful residue
- “More runs will make it work” → identify what would differ and why it can resolve uncertainty
- “The toy result is the theorem” → retain the boundary until a transfer argument is proved
