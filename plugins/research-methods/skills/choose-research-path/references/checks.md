# Decision-relevant checks

Use this as a menu. A small decision may need one or two checks; a high-consequence research allocation may need several. Absence of a complete theory at the beginning is not a defect by itself. Increasing expenditure should produce a more precise promise, a meaningful intermediate result, or an explicit reason why the current obstacle is informative.

## Structural value: four separate questions

| Axis | Concrete question | Evidence that can support it | Common substitution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explanatory force | What dependence, mechanism, or counterfactual becomes understandable? | Derivation, intervention, sensitivity analysis, or an explicit mechanism with tested consequences | A fluent retrospective story |
| Unification | Which previously independent facts follow from one constraint? | Shared derivation plus an accounting of free parameters and auxiliary assumptions | One notation wrapping unrelated fits |
| Conditional necessity | Which alternatives are excluded by the assumptions, and why? | A proof or valid constraint argument with assumptions exposed | “Natural”, “inevitable”, or aesthetically preferred |
| Generativity | What new useful result can this structure produce? | New lemmas, prospective predictions, reusable methods, discriminating experiments, or a precise obstruction | Repeated output, longer explanations, or promises with no changed content |

Predictive utility can matter without causal explanation. A proof can be correct without explanatory transparency. A representation can aid understanding without lowering runtime. These are different achievements, not deficient versions of a single virtue.

## Testability and novelty

State a result that the candidate and a credible rival do not both predict under the same assumptions. Include a modest, differently organized rival: direct enumeration, a table, an empirical predictor, a numerical approximation, or an alternative mechanism.

Record what was known when the candidate was selected. Fitting known data, rediscovering a known identity, and correctly predicting held-out results carry different information. Post-hoc fit may still be useful; label it as such. A complex alternative is not automatically better because it can absorb the anomaly.

A useful negative result may reject a lemma, delimit a regime, disprove an implementation's scaling claim, expose non-identifiability, or identify a necessary assumption. It need not kill the wider program.

## Applicability and transfer

Make the boundary part of the claim:
- object class and quantifiers; existence versus uniqueness; exact versus approximate
- initial/boundary conditions, regularity, conservation, connectedness, symmetry, independence
- finite versus asymptotic size; experimental regime, precision, and population
- hardware, cost model, error tolerance, preprocessing reuse, and output format
- interpolation versus extrapolation; joint support versus separate variable ranges

Ask which bridge takes a toy instance to the target class. Test the first point where the bridge uses an unproved closure, exchange of limits, symmetry restriction, decomposition, or identifiability assumption. A minimal counterexample should violate the actual claim, not a stronger claim that no one made.

An invariant explains what is preserved. Separately check reachability, completeness, termination, and quantitative progress. Preserving something useful does not establish that the procedure reaches its goal.

## Representation audit

Use a meaning-preserving change: invertible coordinates, equivalent definitions, expanded notation, altered units, or an alternative derivation. Track the map and its domain. Which dependencies and invariants survive? Which benefits belong to a specific representation?

Equal input-output functions do not constitute independent confirmation of each other. A noninvertible projection may discard precisely the counterexample. Conversely, a good representation need not look equally simple in every coordinate system. The question is whether it exposes usable structure without silently changing the object or hiding the price.

## The full cost and assumption ledger

Ask where the removed work went:

| Activity | Costs or obligations to include |
|---|---|
| Computation | Construct representation, preprocess, communicate, query, convert back, check, retain history; peak memory, time, energy where relevant, numerical error |
| Proof | Imported theorem assumptions, exact specification, uncovered cases, trusted computation, interface translations, verification effort |
| Experiment | Sample preparation, calibration, nuisance control, measurement precision, intervention side effects, analysis selection |
| Model | Free parameters, data collection, training/selection, leakage, maintenance, residual structure, transfer risk |

For repeated use, compare P + Q·C_new with Q·C_baseline, including memory feasibility and accuracy. Expensive preprocessing can be sensible when real reuse pays for it. A shorter description of the output is not the same task as explicitly producing that output.

Distinguish sunk expenditure from useful surviving assets. Past spending is not evidence of truth; an already verified lemma, reusable instrument, or validated dataset can reduce future cost.

## Decision without a synthetic score

Write the live trade-off in words. If one path dominates for the user's stated objective and constraints, say why. If neither dominates, name the specific choice or missing evidence. Use quantitative estimates only for quantities with defined units and a justified model; do not invent beauty percentages, success probabilities, or weighted quality scores.
