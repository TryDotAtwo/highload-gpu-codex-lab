# Meaning fidelity

For substantive revisions, maintain a source-to-revision map covering definitions, distinctions, formulas, values, units, examples, quantifiers, domains, hypotheses, exceptions, limitations, and open questions. Preserve whether something was observed, proposed, modeled, conjectured, or proved. Added factual claims need support. A detected source error needs an explicit correction trail, not silent amplification or blind preservation.

After reordering episodes, reconstruct the state: which conditions have already changed? Restore an earlier setup explicitly before reusing it. Check each result under its own conditions.

Recompute numerical examples and check signs, dimensions, domains, and admissible operations. Several numerical checks illustrate rather than prove a general identity. A condition must survive relocation into a caption.

For every causal transition identify its warrant. For experiments distinguish the changed factor, comparison, measurement, and expectation. An analogy does not prove a physical law; historical popular sources still require factual checking.

Read whole paragraphs slowly. Resolve demonstratives and pronouns to unambiguous objects, particularly after formulas and figures. Preserve necessary conditions in the prose, leaving organizational audit messages outside it.

Automated searches for numbers or formulas do not establish semantic completeness. Reader rejection is evidence of a problem, but its cause remains unknown unless the feedback identifies it. Do not claim age-specific comprehension without reader evidence.
